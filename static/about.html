Where we are

JobScout is a local pipeline: find jobs from legitimate feeds, score them against your profile, tailor a resume per job with Claude, plan the application from the real form, and let a Claude session fill it in your own Chrome. Everything stays on this machine; nothing is scraped from LinkedIn or Indeed and no bot protection is ever bypassed.

Where the jobs come from: three kinds of employer

Every company runs its hiring on some software (an applicant tracking system, ATS). What matters is how that software exposes the job list.

1. Rented ATS with a documented feed one-line config

Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters, Recruitee, Workable, BambooHR, Breezy. Each publishes the company's open jobs at a public address, full text, no login. Most startups and mid-size tech companies live here. python detect.py "Company" guesses the identifier; the Companies page does it with one click.

2. Rented ATS without a documented feed read the way the page reads it

Workday, Oracle HCM, Phenom, iCIMS. Nobody wrote the feed up, but the careers page loads its jobs from a JSON endpoint with no login and no captcha, and JobScout calls the same endpoint. This is where the large enterprises are: Dell, JPMorgan Chase, Texas Instruments, Cisco, AMD, Charles Schwab, Samsung, Intel, Visa, Applied Materials, CrowdStrike, SailPoint, USAA, GM. Identifiers cannot be guessed from a name, so python detect.py --url <careers page> (or the URL field on the Companies page) fingerprints the page and prints the config line. Recognised but not yet fetchable: Eightfold (refuses calls without its page token: Infineon, Ericsson, PayPal), SuccessFactors (redirects to SSO), Radancy (Arm), Jobvite (Enverus).

3. Own builds behind bot protection never fetched

Apple, Tesla, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Indeed. Reading them would mean pretending not to be a program. That is the line this project does not cross; Adzuna is the only channel for these.

Aggregators discovery, not quality

An aggregator has no jobs of its own; it copies postings from thousands of employers. LinkedIn and Indeed are closed to programs. Adzuna has a free API and is the one window into category-3 employers and staffing agencies. Its cost: 500-character snippets instead of descriptions, 50 newest per query, index dates rather than posting dates, and the same requisition reposted under several agency names. It can be switched off in Settings; its real value is as a seed list of employers to convert into direct boards. Remotive, RemoteOK, The Muse and the monthly Hacker News "Who is hiring" thread add remote and small-company inventory.

Why LinkedIn shows more

Its "Austin" search includes remote-US jobs, every agency repost of the same req, weeks-old postings, and the bot-protected employers above. Here, tick Austin + US remote, widen to 30 days, and include the Infra / Platform / DevOps / Reliability roles alongside SRE to compare like with like.

What gets filtered before you see it

  • Relevance. Boards return every job (sales, legal, design…). Titles matching SRE / DevOps / platform / infra / admin / Kafka / ELK / observability patterns are kept; a job whose title fails is still kept when its description shows at least three distinct ops signals.
  • Location. Non-US is dropped; onsite-only in non-Texas US cities is dropped; global-remote without a clear US signal is dropped (all adjustable in Settings).
  • Age. Aggregator posts older than the limit are dropped; direct boards get their own limit (default 60 days) because companies leave evergreen requisitions open for a year, and the Inbox hides anything older.
  • Work authorization. Postings requiring citizenship or clearance are excluded from the Inbox by default (for a candidate who is work-authorized without sponsorship, those roles are simply closed).
  • Duplicates. The same job from several sources collapses to one card, preferring the direct board's copy with its real posting date.

What's genuinely missed today

  • LinkedIn-only and Indeed-only postings, common for recruiters and staffing agencies.
  • Category-3 employers beyond what Adzuna catches, and category-2 employers not yet in the board list.
  • Contract and C2C reqs. Agencies run staffing platforms (Bullhorn, JobDiva) with no feeds, and most corp-to-corp requirements never reach a website: they move through recruiter email lists and Dice, neither of which has a public API. Adzuna surfaces the public slice (Apex, Insight Global, Robert Half, TCS…).
  • Unusual titles with vague descriptions, and SmartRecruiters boards, which are title-only.

Tailored resumes and applications

  • Start with the ✎ button on a job, or select several and click Tailor resumes (citizen-only and applied jobs are skipped; a per-click cap lives in Settings).
  • Analyze: Claude reads the posting against your skills inventory and asks only about requirements it cannot confirm. A job with open questions waits in the Resumes tab, grouped by job; workers move on to the next job meanwhile. Answers apply to that application only unless you tick "Also remember", the only path into your inventory. Not interested drops a job and its questions in one click.
  • Generate: the resume is rewritten from your master resume and built as a DOCX in resume\output\<Company>\<Role>\<jobId>\ with notes, the source text and a snapshot of the posting. Regenerating keeps history.
  • Q&A and cover letters: long and short versions, within any stated limit, regenerable with your feedback. A cover-letter slot on a form triggers the letter automatically.
  • Plan: reads the real application form and maps every field; multi-select questions are checkboxes, every essay question (required or optional) is drafted, and your edits survive the page refreshing. Applied or rejected jobs leave the pipeline on their own.
  • Fill: a Claude session with the Chrome extension drains the queue in your Chrome, always with the tailored resume, generating a missing cover letter on the spot. Prefill leaves the tab for your Submit; unattended submits only when the live form matches the approved plan. Captchas and account logins are never automated; Workday and Adzuna "Easy Apply" need an account you create once yourself.

Future scope, in rough order of value

  • Automatic board discovery. Follow one Adzuna posting per employer to its landing page, fingerprint it, and propose the config line. Converts the remaining Adzuna-only employers into full-text boards in one run.
  • Scheduled apply runs: prefill in the morning window, unattended at night (the refresh side is scheduled already; the filler still needs a Claude session with Chrome).
  • An "SRE-family" saved filter (SRE + Reliability + Platform + Infra + DevOps + Observability, Austin + US remote, 30 days) so the honest LinkedIn-equivalent view is one click.
  • Eightfold and SuccessFactors fetchers if a clean path appears (Eightfold needs its page token).
  • Contract and C2C stream. A Gmail fetcher over a label you assign to recruiter mailing lists: parse each requirement into a job (title, location, rate, vendor), dedupe reposts, score it, and draft the reply with the tailored resume for you to send. Needs your decision on the LLC route first.
  • A "Run queue" button for captcha-free boards via a browser the app controls itself (Playwright), so filling no longer needs a Claude session for the easy cases.
  • Jooble / Careerjet (free aggregator APIs) and, if it ever feels worth paying for, JSearch (Google for Jobs, which carries most LinkedIn and Indeed inventory).
